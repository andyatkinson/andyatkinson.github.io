The 2026 version of the Ruby on Rails Community Survey results are now live.

Two things caught my eye: just how much PostgreSQL is in production for Rails apps, and the learning resources Rails developers use.

The survey has responses from nearly 1,800 developers.

Among relational SQL databases, [84% of respondents use PostgreSQL in production](https://railsdeveloper.com/survey/2026?q=databases-which-relational-databases-do-you-use-in-production). That's wild! The next one is MySQL at 23%.

![PostgreSQL in production by Rails developers](/assets/images/2026-rails-postgresql.jpg)
<br/>
<small>84% of nearly 1,800 surveyed Ruby on Rails developers use PostgreSQL in production</small>

This reflects a multi-year trend not only in the Ruby on Rails community but also in the broader industry among relational databases.

According to the [DB-Engines Ranking Trend](https://db-engines.com/en/ranking_trend), Oracle, MySQL, and Microsoft SQL Server are all still more popular than PostgreSQL, but declining. In the graph below we can see the growing popularity of PostgreSQL and how it's likely to surpass Microsoft SQL Server soon. MySQL may be next.

## How do Rails developers learn
How do developers learn? Books are holding down a smaller portion of learning resources for developers at 37% for developers in their first two years of learning. For those developers 10% of them felt reading all of a book worked best, while 7% felt that way and they'd read part of a book. This means books still help developers, but less so.

What worked better for them was building and deploying their own applications, which makes sense! That’s how it’s always been for me too. Nothing beats building something yourself and becoming an owner and operator. However, documentation, learning videos, podcasts, books, and blog posts all help support those developers looking to learn new skills.
